Gyms find it hard to turn viewers into buyers. Not because the content is bad — because every post ends in the same ask, and that ask is a membership. It is the biggest thing you sell, put to someone who has known you nine seconds.

31 July 2026 · @rugsbysensei · handmade rugs

People love the rugs. Then they leave.

Twelve videos, about 25,000 views, and a like rate most accounts never reach. Almost nobody followed. The bio said “DM to order” and nothing else — no price, no link — so a stranger who wanted a rug had no idea whether it cost three thousand shillings or thirty.

The recommendation was not to make better videos. It was to put a price and a one-tap link in the bio, and to sell the four rugs already finished before selling “custom” — because custom asks a stranger to pay upfront and wait weeks.

September 2026 · two Nairobi gym groups · published prices

Every plan is a duration.

Two multi-branch Kenyan gym groups, read from their own public price pages and recomputed to a per-month rate — which neither of them publishes, which is why nobody notices what it shows.

Not one plan is a kind of person, and not one is a time of day. A day pass, a month, three, six, twelve. You have four ways to buy the same thing and no way to buy a different thing — so every post has one offer to point at, and it is the biggest one.

Views to Members

A six-week campaign for gyms that already post. I build one thing your members can say yes to from a phone, and the month of content that sells it. Your team shoots it. Your front desk sells it from a script I write.

KSh 25,000 per campaign · one brand

Do the division yourself. If a month at your gym is 6,000, this pays for itself at five members who stay one month. That is the arithmetic on your own price page, not a projection of mine.

  1. Find the strengthYour numbers, plus short review calls to your own members from a script I give you. Not what you assume you are best at.
  2. Build the offerOne simple, named, dated campaign built on that strength — a six-week challenge, small enough to buy from a phone.
  3. Test it on current members firstOne week, before a single post is made. If they will not buy it, we fix it before thirty days of content are built on top of it.
  4. Thirty days of contentFive pillars on a five-day cycle. Every day has an angle and every angle ends at the same offer.
  5. Brief the deskSales scripts, and your existing staff trained to use them, so what the content brings does not leak at the last step.
  6. Read the numbers backAnyone who walks in has to name the offer. For the first time, the posting has a countable end.

If it does not bring you five new subscribers in six weeks, I build the next one free.

What that asks of you. Post at least 24 of the 30 planned posts, have the offer live at the desk from day one, use the script on enquiries, and log everyone who names the offer. Those four are what actually produce the result.
